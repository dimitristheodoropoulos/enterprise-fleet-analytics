Step 5 — Recommendation

Before allocating engineering time: edit distance is a proxy, not ground truth — a user may edit for style, not error. Next step is a native-speaker linguist review of a random sample, not an immediate fine-tuning sprint.
Since I don't speak the affected language, my own next move would be to use an LLM to translate 50 random edits back to English, scan for patterns, then hand the shortlist to a native-speaker linguist — combining AI tooling with human judgment rather than trusting either alone.
Read the methodology in plain language
  1. Surface the pattern. Look at the raw averages — nothing more than "X is higher than Y."
  2. Test the easy explanation first. Re-weight the data so the comparison is fair, then see if the gap survives. Here it didn't move — content mix wasn't the story.
  3. Test the next plausible cause. Sentence length had a real correlation, but the outlier pair's gap remained even after accounting for it — a contributor, not the driver.
  4. Look for a mechanism, not just a correlate. The model version rollout explained the timing directly: every other pair improved sharply after the upgrade, one pair barely moved.
  5. Recommend, but don't overclaim. The metric alone can't distinguish a real quality problem from a stylistic edit — so the recommendation includes a human verification step before committing resources.
